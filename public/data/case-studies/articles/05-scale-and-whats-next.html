Along with the growth in sales come challenges and interesting problems that are always expanding and changing for departments, including Engineering (technology) in general and Operation Product (my current team) in particular. This article aims to introduce these problems, review what the team has solved and share future plans.

Operation Product includes solutions for Operations, Finance and Accounting and Administration within the company, an area that Tiki chose to build almost entirely in-house similar to Amazon.

The biggest problem in Operations, not only in Tiki in particular but in e-commerce in general, is how to deliver products to customers in a fast enough time and at a reasonable cost. However, that is a problem for the future, but in the beginning, the story is simply how to... process orders smoothly first :).

The first way to pick up goods is to print a list of products to be picked up in the order along with a list of shelves that can hold them on paper, and the order handler (picker) sequentially Go get this list. The reason why each product comes with a shelf list is because at that time the system did not manage in detail which products were on which shelves and in what quantities. This picking method is simple to implement but will be slow as the number of orders increases, and depends a lot on the picker's experience and familiarity with the products in the warehouse.

The next improvement step for order processing in particular and Operational solutions in general is to help people "think less" but achieve higher productivity, and this can be done with the help of machine calculations :) This is the time when Tiki begins to use a new order picking system that combines the algorithm for collecting multiple orders (pick by batch), calculating the optimal pick-up route in the warehouse and at the same time putting into use specialized PDAs (handheld computers). used instead of paper. You can see more details about this project here. The time of project completion is also the time when Tiki puts into use the new main warehouse in Ho Chi Minh City, which is 3 times larger than the old warehouse, helping to handle the processing of a larger volume of orders in a space containing more products but faster, which is an important part in the process of scaling up Operations.

However, in order for the above pickup solution to be feasible, another project is also being implemented at the same time and this is also an important foundation for a well-operated system, which is Detailed inventory management. Simply put, if before, the system only knew roughly how many items this product had in stock, possible Which shelves are located? Now, the operator can know exactly at each time, how many items each product has in which location in the warehouse, this is the main data used to calculate the optimal route when picking goods.

Managing inventory accurately is not easy at all. There are two main flows: when receiving goods into the warehouse (inbound), the operator needs to count, confirm the quantity and store the goods on the shelves in the warehouse, when processing orders (outbound), the goods will be removed from the shelves and taken to the packaging area. These are processes that involve many manual operations, so there will be many possibilities for errors, so how to design the interface so that users clearly understand what they need to do, which items to count, what the reference image is, how many, when is it completed, when there is an exception (error, for example, when receiving goods and detecting a missing product), how to handle them are interesting UX issues that the inventory management system needs to handle.

The next step in managing goods accurately, picking up goods from the warehouse faster is delivering goods from the warehouse which also needs to be faster and serve customers on a larger scale. That was when Tiki started working with shipping companies such as Fast Delivery (GHN), Post Office (VNPost) and at the same time built its own delivery team (Tiki Express). Basic features for managing shipping partners are also gradually being deployed, which is the ability to install and configure delivery routes, the system needs to understand which route (detailed to about over 11,000 wards/communes across Vietnam) will be handled by which partner, and record the delivery time from Tiki warehouses to that route according to each partner's package. This information will be used in combination with information about order entry and processing time to obtain an estimated delivery date notified to customers on the Tiki web/app.

Another important part in managing shipping partners is exchanging information about orders. This part will begin when the order is physically delivered and ends when it reaches the customer, in which information such as value, volume, delivery specifications (whether it needs to be checked by both parties or not, whether collection is needed for Cash-on-delivery or not) will be sent by Tiki to the partner, in the opposite direction, information such as the status of the order, where it is going, at what time, and proof of receipt of the customer's goods. (proof-of-delivery) will be sent back to Tiki by the partner. At first, this information was exchanged manually via Excel files, then API integration (this is also an interesting problem because each partner has different technical capabilities for integration) and for Tiki Express, another step forward is the launch of a mobile app for shipping staff, making updating order information more real-time :)

So, after the order is delivered, is the system no longer responsible? The answer is... a long time.

There are still parts like After-Sales, which include processes that allow Tiki to handle customer questions and complaints and resolve them as quickly as possible to ensure the customer experience is not damaged too much if the product is unsatisfactory. How to effectively handle defective warehouses and goods received back from customers is also an interesting problem but is not always given due attention.

It is also the Accounting system when it needs to be recorded exactly (Here it can be understood as down to every penny) the sales of this order and related information such as the product's import price, the product's selling price, Tiki's generated profit, receivables related to the order - for example, collecting money from the delivery partner for COD orders or from the payment gateway for prepaid orders (Account Receivable module). In addition, the Accounting system also needs to manage other information flows such as purchase-related information to know what amounts must be paid, to whom, when to pay (Account Payable), manage other revenues and expenditures (Expense), manage assets, divide budgets between departments (Budget) and finally produce reports to help companies track important indicators related to cash flow, inventory, and profits to optimize reasonable business policies. Although this system is not under the pressure of needing to support orders to be processed as quickly as the Operational systems, there is another pressure: the data needs to be absolutely accurate. This data is received from many different sources (purchase and return orders, purchase and return orders, receipts and payments, etc.), so the possibility of errors or omissions is there. Reconcile and adjustment of this information is a complex but important problem for Accounting in particular and company management activities in general.

What's next?

It can be said that Tiki has passed the stage “Let's make it work first” and is in stage “make it run more optimally”. Interesting problems are being solved and will be solved in the future, both in terms of products and processes

  • The ordering and inventory balancing system is more optimal, helping to optimize capital resources (not storing too much goods with few buyers) while still ensuring customer experience (containing enough goods when customers order). These are projects that are being tested with a number of directions such as Machine Learning combined with traditional optimization algorithms
  • The picking system is smarter, calculating not only based on the optimal distance traveled in the warehouse, but also taking into account the status of the order and giving priority to orders that need to be processed first (for example, customers choose a faster delivery package, or orders are about to miss the committed time).
  • The system suggests a list of items that need to be checked in the warehouse, including calculated products capable There is a discrepancy between system inventory and actual inventory
  • Smarter delivery routing system that calculates optimal routes and delivers orders in real time (like Uber/Grab apps) instead of just delivering orders at the beginning of the day from the warehouse
  • The Finance and Accounting system records full information of all operational flows, reports can access large data (large time set, large number of products) and synchronize with data warehouse.
  • Projects need to be tracked and monitored more closely
  • Recruitment improves both quantity and quality, including recruitment process, on-boarding process, training/coaching process, and performance evaluation process. At the same time, consider opening new tech-hubs to access quality brain matter in the regions
  • The leads in the team also need to have higher standards of quality and transmit this spirit to the team. People need to think critically, solve problems thoroughly and always be curious, explore, stay relevant
